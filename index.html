Chronos Sequential Identity-Preserving Video Generation by Temporal Anchoring

    01Abstract

    Identity-preserving video generation aims to synthesize high-quality and visually realistic videos from textual and visual conditions while faithfully maintaining the subject's identity across diverse motions. Prior methods have extensively introduced reference-guided control signals and identity-aware conditioning to improve facial alignment from reference images. However, improving static identity alignment alone creates a fundamental tension between identity fidelity and temporal controllability, which frequently causes identity drift or motion suppression under large pose variations and multi-segment sequential actions. To address this, we propose Chronos, a framework that unifies single-segment facial identity preservation and multi-segment sequential action generation under a common temporal formulation. Specifically, Chronos integrates Semantic Prompt Anchoring to adaptively refine the user prompt, Visual Frame Anchoring to transform conditions into visually grounded keyframe anchors for visual guidance, and Metric-Guided Candidate Routing to select the highest-quality videos from heterogeneous generators via multi-dimensional evaluation. Extensive evaluations demonstrate the superiority of our approach, which achieved tied first place in Track 1 and first place in Track 2 of the ACM MM 2026 Identity-Preserving Video Generation Challenge, demonstrating its effectiveness across both facial identity preservation and sequential action generation settings.

    02Method

    One temporal formulation for both tracks.

    Given reference images ℐ, a raw prompt P and optional temporal captions 𝒞 = {ct}t=1T, Chronos generates a video that keeps the target identity while following the scene, the actions and their order.

    T = 1Track 1 · facial identity preservation, a single segment
    T > 1Track 2 · sequential actions, keyframe anchors per action phase

    1. Stage 1 · SPA

      Semantic Prompt Anchoring

      A non-intrusive prompt refinement that strengthens identity constraints while keeping the original scene, style and motion.

      • Identity cue extraction. A GPT model reads the reference images and keeps only stable, invariant identity cues H, dropping transient attributes such as clothing and background.
      • Risk-aware prompt adaptation. It estimates the identity-drift risk of the prompt as a score s ∈ {1, 2, 3} and adds concise viewpoint and visibility cues to produce P̂.
      1 Light · minimal correction 2 Medium · identity-aware adjustment 3 High · strong identity protection

    2. Stage 2 · VFA

      Visual Frame Anchoring

      Text alone is ambiguous when the target pose, action or viewpoint departs from the reference. VFA uses an image-to-image operator ΦI2I to turn ℐ, P̂ and an instruction template into visually grounded keyframes.

      • Track 1. A single high-fidelity first-frame anchor A1 initializes image-to-video generation.
      • Track 2. Anchor A1 comes from the first caption c1; each later anchor At is generated from ct and the previous keyframes, in a cascade.
      A1A2⋯AT

    3. Stage 3 · MGCR

      Metric-Guided Candidate Routing

      Generators G1…GK each turn (P̂, 𝒜) into a candidate video. An automatic evaluator scores every candidate on text consistency, identity, subject preservation, motion, visual quality and an LLM-based holistic judgment.

      R(Vk) = Σj λj sj(Vk)

      V* = arg maxVk ∈ 𝒱 R(Vk)

      • Exception-aware review. If the winner falls below a threshold τj on any critical metric, it goes to human review instead of being accepted on its total score alone.

    Chronos is training-free: no model is fine-tuned. The generator pool uses Wan2.2 I2V-14B for both tracks, plus Seedance for Track 1 and Wan FLF2V for Track 2; SPA and VFA use GPT-based models.

    Comparison with existing methods

    Bold marks the best and italics the second-best value in each column. RefAlign and Phantom keep a higher Subject Score (0.80 and 0.72 vs. 0.68) but move less (Motion Amp. 0.29 and 0.21 vs. 0.43); Stand-In moves the most (0.46) but loses the face (Subject 0.24). Chronos balances the two and leads on GMEScore, Aesthetic, Smoothness, Natural and Total.

    Bold marks the best and italics the second-best value in each column. Chronos has the best Subject (0.440), Aesthetic, Smoothness, Natural and Total scores. Stand-In leads on text alignment and motion amplitude but has the lowest Subject Score (0.184); Chronos has the lowest Motion Amplitude and the lowest GMEScore (0.691) on this track.

    Ablation study

    • SPA alone gives a small identity gain on Track 1 (Subject 0.751 → 0.756) and raises Natural Score (0.578 → 0.588).
    • VFA keyframe anchors with an image-to-video generator lift Track 1 Aesthetic (0.50 → 0.58–0.60) and Motion Amp. (0.18 → 0.37–0.50) at some cost in Subject. On Track 2, SPA and VFA together raise Subject from 0.458 to 0.534–0.591.
    • Routing adds +0.019 (Track 1) and +0.011 (Track 2) Total Score over the best single generator, so full Chronos has the best Total on both tracks.

    Bold marks the best and italics the second-best value in each column. Ablations use VACE as the baseline and run on the first 100 cases, a representative subset whose trends match the full evaluation set, so absolute numbers differ from the comparison table.
